A bit more about me...

I'm not going to write a third-person bio. That would be weird. I'm a French developer who moved to the UK in 2008. In 2011, Catherine and I started a Rails consultancy. We grew it to 20 people and over a million in revenue before selling to Amba in 2021.

Now I'm CTO at Amba, running an 8-person dev team building software for elderly care. I train HYROX at 5:30am. I have a hard 4pm rule — phone down, family time, non-negotiable.

I write because I kept seeing advice from navy seals and billionaires performing certainty they don't have. I'm not any of those things. I stopped publishing for a few years while I rebuilt parts of my life and career after the sale. Now I'm starting again, differently.

I'm treating my work as a public CTO lab: change one thing, measure what happens, publish the result. Including the experiments that fail. No productivity theatre, no new system to adopt.

— Nic

What I've learned

Six things I believe after 20 years.

The best CTOs aren't the busiest.

They've designed systems, teams, and lives that don't require heroics.

Boring technology wins.

Rails has been declared dead for 15 years. Still ships faster than most alternatives.

Complexity is a choice.

Most teams don't have scaling problems. They have simplicity problems.

Unexamined meetings become debt.

A recurring meeting nobody questions is a tax on your team's ability to think deeply.

AI made output cheap, not judgement.

Value moved from writing code to owning outcomes. Attention before automation.

Burnout often exposes a systems problem.

If your company needs heroes to function, your systems are broken.
