1/6 In January I froze our tech hiring plan. I wanted to understand what AI was going to do to my eight-person dev team before I added people to it. Seven months later, the answer wasn't what I expected.

---

2/6 I assumed the finding would be which tool won. Wrong. We trialled four coding agents and the output depended on the developer. Some did an OK job. Some trusted the tools too much and sent back mountains of code we had to reject.

---

3/6 The tools were good at writing code. They had no idea how we wanted them to work. So I documented our workflow the way I'd onboard a new team of PMs, analysts, developers and QA: here is how we want you to work with us.

---

4/6 A ticket used to be "Integrate with Sensor X", an API link and ten lines of context. Now the workflow reads the docs against our code, builds a multi-card epic, writes user stories and QA criteria, and helps the developer lock a delivery plan.

---

5/6 Not one line of that is code. It's the work that used to get done badly, or not at all, before anyone opened an editor. Scoping is my job. Delivery planning is the developer's job. The workflow makes that conversation happen every time.

---

6/6 When my CEO asked about productivity, I told him I wasn't interested in 10x. Maybe 1.25, maybe 1.5. The number I care about is how well we solve the problem. 50-odd tickets in, low rejection rate. Full write-up: https://nicalpi.com/blog/ai-measuring-wrong-thing/
