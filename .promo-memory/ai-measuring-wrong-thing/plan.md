# Promo plan: If You're Measuring AI by Speed, You're Measuring the Wrong Thing

## Extracted angles

1. **Thesis**: Measuring AI by speed misses the point. The real gain is in the quality of planning and problem-solving before anyone writes code.
2. **Atomic ideas**:
   - A. AI tools write code fine, but without knowing how your team works they produce mountains of rejects. The fix was documenting the workflow like onboarding a new team.
   - B. The best AI output on this team isn't code — it's the ticket writing, scoping and QA criteria that used to get skipped.
   - C. A silent token bug got traced and fixed properly (not patched) because the workflow forced the planning conversation. The code was the easy part.
   - D. "I told my CEO I wasn't interested in 10x. Maybe 1.25, maybe 1.5." The number that matters is precision, not speed.
   - E. Scoping is the CTO's job, delivery planning is the developer's job — the workflow makes the planning conversation happen whether or not the developer would have had it alone.
3. **Sharpest stat**: "The 50-odd tickets we've built with the workflow and the low rejection rate" — seven months, eight-person team.
4. **Core tension**: Speed vs. precision. Everyone sells AI on 10x productivity. This post says: wrong metric.
5. **Quotable lines**:
   - "I didn't want us to push more tickets. I wanted us to spend more relative time on a ticket, and when a ticket was pushed, for it to be solving more and better."
   - "Not one line of that is code."
   - "Scoping is my job. Delivery planning is the developer's job."
6. **Personal angle**: Froze hiring in January to understand what AI would do to an eight-person team. Seven months later, the answer wasn't what he expected. Also: returning to writing after months away.
7. **Contrarian take**: "I told my CEO I wasn't interested in 10x" directly contradicts the dominant AI narrative. Most AI content promises speed. This says: speed is the wrong metric.

## Nic's direction

- Lead angles: #7 (contrarian — not chasing 10x), #6 (personal — froze hiring, coming back to writing), #2B and #2E (specifics — ticket quality, planning as the real gain)
- Target audience: broader than the post — CTOs, tech leads, but also founders, engineering managers, non-technical leaders dealing with AI pressure
- Desired reaction: mix — reconnection on launch day (I'm back), debate through the rest (10x is wrong)
- Off-limits: no naming Stuart or quoting him directly. The CEO conversation is "my CEO asked about productivity" without the name or the 10x joke. "I assumed, and I was wrong" is fine.
